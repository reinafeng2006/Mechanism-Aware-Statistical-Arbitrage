## 5. Scientific lessons

1. **Relationship fit ≠ stationarity persistence.** A lower out-of-sample prediction loss compares specified conditional relationships; it does not establish a stable stationary spread or a lasting mean-reversion mechanism.
2. **Stationarity/statistical abnormality ≠ tradable convergence.** Statistical displacement need not persist until executable entry or resolve within the economic horizon. Neither its frequency nor a stationary-looking historical series identifies capturable gains.
3. **Signal model ≠ executable basket.** Regression/factor coordinates need not be traded assets. Actual proxy instruments, exposure matching, borrow and simultaneous execution require their own evidence.
4. **Model residual ≠ tradable target.** H-C introduces an intentional model-to-trade difference plus unwanted proxy error. Target B and both sign checks keep the traded displacement and C4 exit in the same actual-basket coordinate.
5. **Transaction-cost assumptions cannot substitute for execution evidence.** A basis-point deduction cannot establish fills, late fills, partial inventory, legal unwind, duration, recall or settlement obligations.
6. **Borrow is part of the strategy state.** Quantity, commitment validity, fees, collateral and recall determine whether the basket can exist and how it exits; they cannot be appended after measuring returns.
7. **Missing economic evidence means unavailable.** Missing costs are not zero costs, unknown obligations are not flat positions, and incomplete paths are not complete return observations. Failure costs belong to attempts even without an admitted episode.
8. **Equations and evidence requirements should precede acquisition.** Freezing the estimand, coordinates, denominator, states, execution and inference before development makes missing identification visible and prevents data availability or outcomes from silently selecting a different strategy.

These are methodological distinctions supported by the project design and qualification record, not newly estimated causal claims.
