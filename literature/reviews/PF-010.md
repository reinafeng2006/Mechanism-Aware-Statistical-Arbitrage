# PF-010 — Qureshi & Zaman (2024)

## Current G1 question(s) and decision trigger

A-P3. Determine whether global matching/overlap control contributes to pair generalizability or instead governs portfolio concentration.

## Full-text admission and version/provenance

arXiv:2403.07998v1, March 2024, full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07998. Reviewed 2026-09-03; citation and researcher-authored notes retained only.

## Construct / method / relevance

Starting with weighted candidate cointegration edges, the paper uses maximum-weight graph matching to select a portfolio with no shared assets. The output is an overlap-constrained portfolio allocation/governance object, not a new economic relationship measure. Role: P/R at portfolio-construction governance level.

## Identification, limitations, and claim reconciliation

The paper **supports a narrow global-governance claim**: shared assets can create concentration/covariance in a multi-pair portfolio under the paper's model, and matching can impose exclusivity. It does not establish that excluded or selected pairs are economically related, statistically valid, stable, or generalizable as individual pairs. The reported performance/cost results cannot validate pair relationships and must not influence G2 relationship choice.

## Logic chain and downstream boundary

Premise: independent local pair selection can reuse the same asset. Measurement: candidate-edge graph and matching constraint. Observed evidence: theoretical covariance result and S&P 500 implementation under cointegration assumptions. Inference: overlap may be a portfolio-level governance consideration. Final claim: global matching is a candidate constraint only after pair validity is separately established; it is not relationship validity.

## Missing link, validation test, project testability

Missing link: whether exclusivity versus controlled overlap improves risk governance after company-aware, PIT-valid pairs are defined. G2 may distinguish individual-pair validity from portfolio overlap policy; G4/G5 later require frozen portfolio/risk constraints. No graph algorithm, edge weight, exclusivity rule, or portfolio objective is selected.

## Permitted and prohibited use

Permitted: candidate global overlap/concentration governance concept. Prohibited: using matching to certify a pair, adopting maximum-weight matching, cointegration edge weighting, performance ranking, or trade implementation.
